The Founder’s Story

MY STORY

The life, struggle, faith, dreams and questions that led Sayas Lama toward cinema—and eventually toward THE HUNK.

This is not a story about having everything. It is a story about being given life, carrying responsibility, holding on to a dream, and trying to make something meaningful from the life I was given.

01 — A SIMPLE PERSON

My name is Sayas Lama. I am a very ordinary person. I do not have a degree in the name of education. If you call me uneducated, however, I would say that I have one ability within me: I can look at what is happening in the world, search for its causes, and try to understand and adopt possible solutions. That does not mean that nobody can fool me. I am not someone who can never be deceived. But until today, no one has cheated me and taken something from me through manipulation or trickery. If I see another person’s pain and hardship, I try to understand it as if it were my own and help in whatever way I can, without knowing whether I will ever receive anything in return. If someone mistakes that compassion for cleverness on their part, then I sincerely pray that they will have enough to live on for the rest of their life. 🙏

02 — WHERE I STAND

My family’s situation has never been extremely bad, nor has it been especially comfortable. By God’s grace, I have never had to bow before someone or beg in order to eat. For that blessing and grace, I will always remain grateful to my God, until my final breath. 🙏

03 — THE DAY I WAS BORN

When I speak about my birth, I have to begin with pain. As I said above, when I was born, both my mother and I were surrounded by immense hardship and suffering. Perhaps I was born into a situation where I was expected to die. But God was with me, and I survived. Yet in the place where I might have died, someone else lost their life in my place.

04 — A SMALL WATERMILL

I was born in a small watermill in Damarpur village, Amlekhganj. My family made a living there by grinding grains such as rice and mustard for people from the surrounding villages. That same watermill became the place where my life began.

05 — MY MOTHER’S THREE DAYS

Every woman who gives birth goes through pain that cannot simply be described. But what that pain truly feels like can only be known by the woman who experiences it; no two people necessarily experience it in exactly the same way. When I was born, my mother was overwhelmed by extraordinary pain and suffering. After giving birth to me, she remained unconscious for three days. No one could tell whether she was alive or dead. She lay unconscious beside the newborn child, holding that child close to her.

06 — THE DREAM

According to my mother, because she had been unconscious for three days, everyone had almost reached the conclusion that she had died. But the moment she regained consciousness after those three days, the first thing she asked was, “Where is my baby?” During those three days, she says she was in a very deep sleep and trapped inside a frightening dream. Three unfamiliar, terrifying, very tall figures with extremely long hair were chasing her. Their faces could not be seen at all. My mother was running for her life, begging them, “My baby is here. Please do not do anything to me.” But those three frightening figures showed no mercy and kept chasing her.

07 — THE POND

Finally, in the dream, my mother fell into a pond and sank so deeply that she could no longer be seen. The three figures searched and waited for a long time. When they could not find her, they eventually turned around and left. My mother, meanwhile, slowly looked out from the water, watching and waiting for the moment they would disappear so she could escape. After making sure they were gone, she tried to climb out but could find nothing to hold on to. Then she found a thin vine growing in the pond. Using that tiny vine for support, she slowly began to pull herself out. But just as she was about to escape, those terrifying figures appeared again—they had been hiding nearby and waiting. The moment they saw her, they began chasing her again, saying in effect, “So, you were here after all.” As she ran, she suddenly woke up. Three days had passed.

08 — A MOTHER WHO TASTED DEATH

In that way, my mother gave birth to me after, in her experience, living through what felt like death for three days. Someone who reads this story tomorrow may not feel much about it. I have nothing to say against that. But the person who lived through it, who faced it directly, is the only one who can truly know the pain and suffering of that wound.

09 — THE SECOND TIME I SHOULD HAVE DIED

Earlier I said that a few years after my birth, perhaps I was supposed to die—but another child died instead. This is what happened. Because I was born in that watermill, I used to play around it all the time. One day my mother took me somewhere else for a walk. That very day, an older girl who had been born only a few weeks or months before me went to the place where I normally played. She fell into the watermill and died. She became trapped in the main wheel that turned the mill, and her body remained there.

10 — THE CHILD THE VILLAGE THOUGHT WAS ME

The villagers thought the child who had drowned was me. Everyone was saying that the child who had died was Sayas. Then, at that very moment, they saw me walking back while holding my mother’s hand. The whole village was shocked. They began asking: if the child trapped and killed inside the watermill was not this boy, then whose child had died there? Perhaps I would have died that day. Whether it was God’s grace upon me, or whether that child’s death became a sacrifice in my place, I still do not know the answer. It remains a mystery to me.

11 — THE GIFT OF LIFE

Whatever the explanation may be, I believe that was the moment I received what I can call a second birth—or another profound act of God’s grace. Because the most precious possession and blessing any living being has is life. Whether a person is materially wealthy or crushed by poverty, life itself is everything. What we gain, earn, build, or lose afterward is the result of our own abilities and weaknesses—not because God creates people with different amounts of worth.

“The most precious possession and blessing any living being has is life.”

12 — WHY I AM GRATEFUL

That is how my life began. That is why, to this day, I thank my God every day for giving me this life. The responsibility of decorating and shaping this life is now mine. I believe God has given every human being the ability to do that equally.

13 — THE DREAM OF CINEMA

The child who did not die that day has now grown into a young man with a dream: to understand cinema and to help others understand it. That desire has been with me since childhood, but I could never openly tell anyone about it because, given my circumstances and position, cinema felt very far away.

14 — A FIELD THAT FELT FAR AWAY

Everyone knows that entering this field has often seemed to require one of three things: money, connections, or the ability to flatter and hold on to someone’s feet. I was miles away from all three. Of course, it is possible to study cinema, but studying requires money. At that time, my mother and father worked as daily-wage laborers. Their income was barely enough for our morning and evening meals, rent, my schooling, and one new set of clothes each year. Where could extra money come from for me to study cinema?

15 — THE POSTERS ON THE WALL

When I was a child, I used to see Nepali movie posters covering the walls. Those posters planted the same desire inside me. And when Friday came and I could watch a Nepali film on Shangri-La Channel, I would imagine myself as Rajesh Hamal. 🤭 Hindi films were shown on Nepal Television every Saturday, and no matter how good a Hindi film was, my heart and desire were always with Nepali cinema.

16 — WATCHING FILMS FROM A LANDLORD’S HOME

To watch a Nepali film in a cinema hall, I needed a ticket, and a ticket required money—money that a child like me simply did not have. I had seen the struggle and hard work of my parents, so even asking for money for snacks at school felt difficult and frightening. Asking them for money to watch a film was something I simply could not bring myself to do. Much later, when the Shangri-La Channel began showing Nepali films every Friday at nine at night, my mother, my sister and I would go to our landlord’s home to watch them. That love for cinema has stayed with me to this day.

17 — THE RESPONSIBILITY I CARRY

Even now, I have not been able to speak openly about my dream, because the same obstacles remain: money, and the fact that I have no one in the cinema field who is personally connected to me. Today there are far more schools and theatre spaces where someone can study cinema than there were before. But when you are the eldest son in a family with limited means, you learn that responsibility must come before your own wishes. I have carried that weight until today, while carrying my love for cinema alongside it. I do not know when I will finally be able to put down that burden of desire and longing.

18 — A NEW DOOR OPENED

But there is one beautiful thing about reaching 2026. To study cinema and understand it, I no longer have to attend a school or theatre, nor do I have to fold my hands before someone and beg them to teach me. AI systems such as ChatGPT and Gemini can now teach people many subjects affordably and repeatedly, almost like having a book and teacher available whenever you need them.

19 — WHY I AM THANKFUL FOR AI

I want to thank ChatGPT from the depths of my heart. It has given me knowledge, help, and encouragement in many areas—things that would have required enormous amounts of time and money if I had needed to learn them from a person, school, or college. I know AI has put many jobs at risk and may affect many more in the future. But for people like us who have limited financial means, I believe it has, at least for now, proved to be something close to a blessing. Sometimes I feel that God heard the prayer of a poor man like me: I can ask, again and again, about the things I want to learn without having to beg anyone, and I can learn at my own will.

20 — THE WAY I CHOOSE TO STAND

I have a particular nature. I am not someone who will hold on to someone’s feet or flatter them just to get something or earn something. Whatever I become, whatever I achieve, I want it to come through my own ability and qualification. I have a stubborn side that says I will never beg before someone to receive what I believe I can work for. People who know me and understand this side of me sometimes call me arrogant. When I look honestly at myself, however, I do not think I am arrogant—I think I am stubborn.

21 — FAITH BEHIND THE STUBBORNNESS

If someone calls me arrogant, they may. It does not hurt me. But even if my stubbornness looks like arrogance, it does not come from wealth, possessions, or academic qualifications. The confidence I carry comes from my true God. I have complete trust in Him—that He will not leave me hungry, will not leave me naked, and will not put me to shame. Whether my wishes and dreams come true or not is something tomorrow will reveal.

22 — WHY I AM THANKFUL FOR THIS LIFE

For the bitter, sweet, strange, difficult, beautiful and deeply educational experiences that have filled this precious life, I once again thank God from the depths of my soul. 😍 And for the people who created AI, I also offer my heartfelt thanks on behalf of all of us who have limited means. 🙏

23 — WHY THE HUNK BEGAN

I am still learning about cinema myself. And I began the journey of THE HUNK because I want to make the things I learn easier for other people to understand too. Let us see where THE HUNK’s journey reaches tomorrow.

24 — THE ROAD AHEAD

I have complete faith and trust in my God. I believe that if He saved me from the edge of death, if He pulled me away from the hands of death and brought me to this day, then there must be a reason and a purpose behind this precious life. Perhaps that purpose is to become sharper in cinema through THE HUNK, and perhaps to contribute something meaningful to the Nepali film world.
